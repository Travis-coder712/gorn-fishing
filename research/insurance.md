# Insurance research - Caribbean 205 Commodore (Vic, Oct 2026)
Tags: [V] = verified on a fetched page; [E] = estimate / forum anecdote / inference. No quote forms were filled in.

## Headline
Insurer websites publish almost no eligibility detail (age cap, hp cap, min premium). You need to phone or get a quote. Expect mainstream insurers (RACV, NRMA/CGU, Youi) to need a report or decline for a 1980s hull. Specialists (Club Marine, Nautilus, brokers) are the realistic route.

## 1. Insurer-by-insurer
- RACV (underwriter IMA; distributor only): fibreglass boats over 10 yrs "may need" a report from the last 5 yrs [V]. Trailer included [V]. Liability "up to $10m in certain circumstances" [V]. Agreed value offered [V]. Cruisers/cuddy cabins covered [V]. Max age, hp, excess not published. https://www.racv.com.au/insurance/boat-insurance.html
- Club Marine (Allianz): covers boat, motor, trailer, gear in one policy; lay-up option; "minimum premiums may apply"; flexible excess [V]. Lay-up discount 5%/month if kept in garage/shed/fenced; NCD up to 25% [V, via Finder]. Under-25 age excess [V]. Age/hp limits not published. Forum reports they dropped older boats: a 1980 boat refused renewal after 6 claim-free years [E, Seabreeze, undated]. https://www.clubmarine.com.au/boat-insurance ; https://www.finder.com.au/car-insurance/boat-insurance/club-marine-boat-insurance
- NRMA / CGU (IAG): PDS and TMD exist (agreed value, choice of excess) but the PDS fetch was blocked (403). Not verified for age or hp. Forum rule of thumb: most insurers cap around 30-35 yrs, then want a seaworthiness certificate or survey [E]. Some insurers scale down parts cover from age 20 to 40 (up to 80% off) [E]. https://nrma.com.au/documents/boat/pds ; https://iag.com.au/tmd/TMDBO002
- Nautilus Marine (Melbourne, IC Frith-backed): trailerable powerboats and runabouts "subject to underwriting acceptance criteria" [V]. Forum: some older boats accepted, some "too old"; declined a 1990 cruiser (mooring case) [E]. Phone 1300 780 533 [V]. https://www.nautilusinsurance.com.au/
- Youi: three levels (comprehensive, TPFT, third party property only); TPPO liability up to $10m [V]. Seabreeze thread titled "Important Youi insurance changes for old boats" (403, unread) suggests tightening for old boats [E]. https://www.youi.com.au/watercraft-insurance/articles/a-first-time-boat-owners-guide-to-boat-insurance
- BoatSafe: boatsafe.com.au redirects to a used-boat inspection business, not an insurer [V]. Could not confirm an insurer by that name. Treat as not applicable.
- AAMI, Budget Direct, Sun Marine: no usable eligibility info found. Not confirmed they write boats (AAMI/Budget unverified). Ask a broker (e.g. Topsail, who reportedly dropped some boats [E]: https://topsailinsurance.com.au/).
- Not found for any insurer: published 200hp limits, named-operator rules, co-owner handling, or ashore-only cover for a non-running boat. Everything below is unverified.
  - 200hp: likely fine at a specialist but may attract a higher excess or rating [E].
  - Co-owners: usually all owners are named insureds, or the registered owner is named with interests noted. Ask for "all five owners noted, survivorship agreement" up front [E].
  - Operator: expect licensed skipper plus experience-based excess; under-25 excess is typical [V for Club Marine].
  - Non-running boat: lay-up / ashore-only cover is a standard product concept (Club Marine lay-up [V]). Insurers may ask for the motor to be declared "not operational" and exclude cover on the water [E].

## 2. Premiums
No reliable current data for this boat. Reported figures are old or for much smaller boats:
- Club Marine "$300 to tens of thousands" per year [V, Finder].
- 2004 forum: ~$260/yr for 14ft tinnie, 30hp, $100 excess; $192 comprehensive for 17ft 50hp tinnie with 6-month lay-up [V, but 2004 data]. https://www.exploroz.com/forum/17705/boat-insurance-where-and-how-much-to-pay
- My estimate for this boat ($8-15k, old cruiser, 200hp): roughly $600-1,500/yr comprehensive on water; roughly $300-700 ashore/lay-up; third-party-only cheaper [E]. Treat as a guess; there may be a minimum premium.

## 3. Compulsory?
- Not compulsory in Victoria [V]. Registration "doesn't include any insurance cover", government recommends it. Powered vessels must be registered and operators licensed [V]. https://www.youi.com.au/youi-news/a-first-time-boat-owners-guide-to-boat-insurance
- Uninsured exposure: you are personally liable for injury or property damage you cause (collision, wash, skier injury, wreck removal). Liability is unlimited without cover; typical policies offer up to $10m [V]. Shared across 5 co-owners and the registered owner, who is the most exposed in practice [E].
- Home and contents: not verified for boats. Insurers sell boat insurance as separate products; assume home policies exclude watercraft liability and often boats and trailers. Confirm with your home insurer in writing [E].

## 4. Recommendation [E]
- Now (being fixed, on trailer at home): ask Club Marine or a broker for lay-up / "ashore" cover covering fire, theft, storm and trailer, with the motor declared non-running. If the quote is a poor deal, a cheaper fallback is fire and theft only.
- Once on the water: comprehensive or third-party property plus fire and theft with at least $5-10m liability. For a $8-15k boat, third-party property with agreed hull value is the cheapest sensible option. Market-value-only is cheaper but payout is uncertain on an old hull.
- Do not launch uninsured: liability is the real risk, not hull value.
- Documents insurers will likely want: recent photos (hull, deck, cabin, motor, trailer, HIN plate, registration), purchase invoice or valuation, mechanic's report or invoice showing the motor works once recommissioned, trailer details, storage address. Possibly a survey or seaworthiness report: an inspection for a 30+ yr fibreglass boat would likely be required by RACV or CGU [V for RACV >10yrs; E for the rest].
- Ask each insurer directly: age cap, hp limit, minimum premium, excess, ashore-only for non-running boat, how 5 owners are named, whether registered-owner-only title is a problem, survivorship agreement recognised.
