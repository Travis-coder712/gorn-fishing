## 1. International Marine / Caribbean — the builder

- **Founded 1958 by Arch (Archie) Spooner**, a Tom Piper canned-food businessman. The business started as **International Plastics** and in the 1950s built copies of British fibreglass boats. [V] [boatsales obituary of Barry Spooner](https://www.boatsales.com.au/editorial/details/barry-spooner-the-builder-of-bertram-and-caribbean-boats-dies-128793/)
- **1963: Bertram licence.** Richard Bertram licensed International Marine of Scoresby to build Bertram designs. In the first year, Archie and six staff built 85 boats. [V] [Wikipedia – Richard Bertram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Bertram), [Trade-a-Boat Caribbean buyer's guide](https://www.tradeaboat.com.au/news-reviews/6984-caribbean-buyer-s-guide)
- **Barry Spooner** (Arch's son) ran the Scoresby factory from 1971. In 2001 he handed over to his son **Richard**, who still runs it today on the same site where the first boat was moulded. [V] (boatsales obituary; Trade-a-Boat)
- **Bertram → Caribbean.** The big boats carried the Bertram name until **1989**, when the licence ended and they were rebranded Caribbean. [V] (boatsales). The *Caribbean* name was already in use on the smaller trailer boats long before that: 1968 Caribbean Galaxy, 1970s Catalina and Invader, 1980 Caribbean 205 Commodore. [V] (listings below; [ANMM photos of Caribbean Catalina, 1974](https://collections.sea.museum/objects/62670/black-and-white-negative-number-32a-depicting-a-caribbean-ca))
- **Pride.** "Pride" was a 1960s–70s brand with a 14-model range in 1968, and photos exist of a "Pride Caribbean Navigator". Ausfish posters say **Pride, Bertram and Caribbean were all under the International Marine banner**. [F] ([1968 trade clipping, Victorian Collections PDF](https://victoriancollections.net.au/media/collectors/52f0757d9821f40464f73def/items/6a4dc78c303b2af2929a1449/item-media/6a4dc79f303b2af2929a1639/original.pdf); [ANMM Pride negatives](https://collections.sea.museum/objects/61203/black-and-white-negative-number-4a-depicting-image-of-pride))
- **Output and reputation:** roughly 50,000–60,000 boats built. Best known for the Bertram/Caribbean 25/26, the Caribbean 35 (launched as the Bertram 35 in 1970 and still built), the 40/42/45/47/49 flybridges and the 2400. The **Reef Runner** (March 1995) replaced the Bertram 20 and gained a cult following. Caribbeans are regarded as tough, conservative, sea-kindly boats with strong resale. [V] ([Trade-a-Boat Reef Runner](https://www.tradeaboat.com.au/news-reviews/10020), [boatsales Reef Runner](https://www.boatsales.com.au/editorial/details/caribbean-reef-runner-8974/))
- **Status:** **still operating and still Spooner-family owned**, at 1278 Ferntree Gully Rd, Scoresby, phone (03) 9763 7233. [V] ([BIA Vic directory](https://www.biavic.com.au/directory/2024/6/25/caribbean-boats-international-marine))
- **Riviera connection:** I found nothing linking it to Riviera. Riviera is a separate Gold Coast builder. A "1991 Caribbean Riviera" 5.7 m cuddy is on boatsales right now, so "Riviera" was also a Caribbean **model name**, which is a likely source of the confusion. [V]
- **Owners' groups:** I found no dedicated Caribbean small-boat owners' club. The most useful history threads are on **Ausfish** (e.g. "Caribbean ????" thread 133762, "is this a Pride 21?" 141921). The factory is reportedly **helpful with build specs if you quote the serial number**. [F]

## 2. The Commodore model

What I could establish:

| Item | Finding | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Name | Sold as **Caribbean 205 Commodore** / "Commodore 20.5" / "Commodore 6.1" | [V] listings |
| Length | 6.1 m (20'0"–20'6" nominal; "205" = 20.5 ft) | [V] |
| Beam | ~2.4 m | [F] snippet |
| Hull weight | Forum: **750–900 kg** (bare hull). Another snippet: 1,400 kg "dry" (probably a sterndrive package) | [F] |
| Hull history | Forum: **1971–78 hull** had a crease/chine in the sides, only 2 full strakes and ~18° deadrise at the transom. **Changed ~1978 to smooth sides and a deeper V** | [F] |
| Twin | Ausfish: the 6.2 m **Pride President** "was also sold as the Caribbean Commodore" (both have twin front windows and 20.5 decals). Written up by Bill Dennis in *Fishing & Boating* no. 127 (c.2006) | [F] |
| Power | Most surviving Commodores listed are **MerCruiser sterndrive/inboard** (180 hp etc.). Outboard versions exist (WA refit with 150 hp) | [V]/[F] |
| Layout | Half/full cabin with lockup door, V-berth (2 adults), Porta Potti under the cushions or between berths, no real galley. Hardtop with clears, rear platform, underfloor tank (~150 L in WA example) | [V] listings, [F] tank |
| Years | At least **1980 and 1984** examples are confirmed by listing. A model from ~1971 to the mid/late 1980s | [V]/[E] |

**What "P209" means is not verified.** My best guesses [E]:
- It is an International Marine internal model/mould code.
- "P" may be **Pride** (the twin hull was sold as the Pride President).
- "209" is either a design number or 20'9" overall including the bowsprit/platform.

**Action:** ring Caribbean on (03) 9763 7233 and quote "Model P209, serial 2513". They may give you build year, original power and spec sheet.

**Check this on our boat:** given how many Commodores were sterndrive, look at the transom. A blanked-off sterndrive cut-out, or a bolted-on pod, means a **conversion**, which matters for transom integrity. The 200 hp max-rating plate suggests a factory outboard version, but confirm. [E]

**How it differs from other models [E]:**
- **Caribbean 2000 / Bertram 20:** a different, Bertram-derived deep-V (~23–24°) design. It is the more offshore-oriented hull and was replaced by the Reef Runner in 1995.
- **Commodore:** an in-house family overnighter with more cabin and less deadrise.
- **Caribbean 21** (1985 restored example listed): also a different, later hull.

**Original price:** not found.

### Component values [E]
- Tandem galvanised Dunbier trailer in usable condition: **$2,500–4,500** on its own.
- Old Yamaha 200 V6 carby 2-stroke:
  - non-running: $300–1,000 (parts/core)
  - running, healthy compression: $1,500–3,500
- Lowrance Elite-5 HDI, winch, bow rail: maybe $500–800 combined in resale terms.

### Valuation ranges [E]

| Scenario | Private-sale value | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) As-is, motor not running** | **$4,000–8,000** | Trailer + hull + gear, discounted for an unknown motor and unknown transom/stringers. Below $5k if the deck or transom is soft |
| **(b) Motor runs well after service** | **$9,000–15,000** | Sound hull, rego'd, ready to fish. Dealer asks of $20–23k include ORC, dealer margin and warranty |
| **(c) New 4-stroke repower** | **Market value $22,000–30,000** | But it costs boat + ~$26–33k to get there. **You'll never recoup a new-motor repower on resale** |

**Critique of "$11–18k turn-key complete package":**
- It's about right for a **running, sound-hulled** example.
- $18k is dealer money. $11–15k is realistic private money.
- It's **too high for the boat as it sits**, with a non-starting 30–40-year-old V6 2-stroke and an un-inspected transom.
- Mates' rates for the as-is boat should be in the **$3k–8k** zone, depending on what the inspection finds.

### Repower costs (AUD)
Brisbane Yamaha 2026 list prices, engine only [V] ([source](https://www.brisbaneyamaha.com.au/yamaha-top-of-the-range-4-stroke/)):
- F115XB $17,075

Rigging adds roughly **$3,000–6,000** [E]: controls, gauges, harness, prop, possibly hydraulic steering, install. Fitted totals:
- **F150 ~$26–30k**
- **F175 ~$29–33k**

Suzuki DF140/150 and Mercury 150 FourStroke are typically a few thousand cheaper. A good **second-hand 4-stroke 115–150 (~$9–16k + rigging)** is the value play. [E]

**Minimum sensible hp [E]:**
- **~115 hp** for the bay with 2–3 people.
- **140–150 hp** is the sweet spot for four adults plus gear.
- 175–200 is not needed.
- Watch transom weight: an F150 is ~216 kg vs ~184 kg for the old 200 2-stroke. Also confirm shaft length (likely 25"/X).

**Verdict [E]:** legal and workable *only if the rig weighs under 2,000 kg*. It's likely to be close. **Weigh it at a public weighbridge** (~$20–30) loaded as you'd tow it, and tow with **fuel low and gear in the car**.
- The Carnival is FWD. Wet ramps at Port Phillip launch sites with a ~2-tonne boat will be marginal, so expect wheelspin on steep or slimy ramps.
- Check the trailer's ATM plate and the brakes. Tandem trailers over 750 kg need working brakes; >2,000 kg ATM needs breakaway brakes.
- GCM wasn't found in the sources. Check the Carnival's owner's manual so that car + passengers + trailer stay within it.

## 5. Good for / not good for

**Good for [E/F]:**
- **Port Phillip Bay** fishing and overnighting:
  - snapper, whiting, squid
  - Mud Islands / Portsea runs in moderate conditions
- Family cruising, Western Port, Gippsland Lakes.
- The hardtop and lockup cabin are great for Melbourne weather.
- Owners of similar Caribbeans report they handle a following sea well.

**Not good for:**
- Bass Strait / outside the Heads except on calm, settled days with experienced crew. At ~18–20° deadrise this is an inshore/bay boat, not a Reef Runner/Bertram 20.
- The Rip demands respect in any 6 m boat.
- Long range on a budget: the 2-stroke is thirsty.

**Fuel burn, Yamaha 200 V6 carby 2-stroke:**
- Cruise (~4,000–4,200 rpm): **~35–42 L/h** [F] ([continuouswave](https://www.continuouswave.com/ubb/Forum1/HTML/015505.html), RIBnet threads)
- WOT: **~75–81 L/h** (81 L/h max quoted) [F]
- A modern F150 cruising: ~20–25 L/h [E]

**Speed [E]:**
- cruise 22–26 knots
- top ~35–40 knots, depending on load and prop

**Range [E]:** ~0.55–0.6 nm per litre at cruise. With ~150 L and a one-third reserve, that's **~55 nm practical**, which is plenty for the bay. At ~$2.20/L, a 2-hour day of running costs ~$150–200 in fuel plus oil.

## 6. Common problems and what to inspect

1980s Caribbean/Bertram trailer boats use **plywood transom, stringers and floor, glassed in, with foam fill**. On 40-year-old hulls these are the big-ticket items [F]:
- Ausfish/boatdesign threads on 20–23 ft Bertram/Caribbeans report **wet transoms, rotten stringers and waterlogged foam (wettest at the stern)** as common.
- The Captain's Caribbean Invader rebuild found the stringers "completely rotten" and the transom soft. ([The Captain](https://thecaptain.tv/project-boats/old-school-caribbean-rebuild/), [boatdesign thread](https://www.boatdesign.net/threads/23-bertram-carribian-stringers-and-transom.44587/page-2))

**Inspection checklist:**
1. **Transom:**
   - With the motor trimmed down, push/pull the leg hard. Watch for flex or cracking around the transom bolts and splashwell.
   - Tap the inside with a small hammer: a dull thud means wet.
   - Look for weeping at the bolt holes.
   - Stress cracks at the transom corners are bad.
2. **Floor/deck:** walk it, especially the cockpit near the tank hatch. Soft or spongy means rot.
3. **Stringers/foam:** pull the bung after rain and see how long it drains. A boat that is **heavier than expected** on the weighbridge suggests wet foam.
4. **Sterndrive-to-outboard conversion?** Look for a patched cut-out in the transom.
5. **Hardtop and windows:**
   - Check leaks at the hardtop-to-hull joint and the sliding-window channels/seals.
   - Check for water stains or rot in the cabin lining and bunks.
   - Hose-test it.
6. **Hull:** gelcoat crazing, keel damage from the trailer, osmosis blisters (less common in trailer boats).
7. **Fuel tank:** old aluminium underfloor tanks corrode. Check the sender, the lines, the filter (water separator) and **fuel age** (old fuel is a common no-start cause).
8. **Trailer:** springs, brakes, bearings, rollers, rust at the drawbar/axles, ATM plate.
9. **Motor (Yamaha 200AETO, international model code, 1980s):**
   - **No spark:** check the cheap stuff first:
     - kill-lanyard switch and key switch (stop-circuit short to ground)
     - then the pulser and charge coils (stator)
     - then the CDI units and coils
     - these V6s have two banks, and losing spark on one bank is a known stator fault. ([iboats](https://forums.iboats.com/threads/no-spark-on-1993-yami-200-txrr.361233/))
   - Once it fires, get a **compression test** (all 6 within ~10%).
   - Check the **Precision Blend oil-injection pump and warning buzzer**, carb condition, water pump/impeller and tell-tale.
   - Parts for this vintage are getting scarce and expensive.

**Cheap, high-impact refit ideas [E]:**
- cut-and-polish the gelcoat, and refresh the pinstripes and "Commodore" decal
- new cabin upholstery and marine vinyl
- LED lighting
- new hardtop clears and window seals
- non-skid paint instead of carpet
- rod holders, baitboard, VHF, a 9" sounder
- a lithium cranking battery or a dual-battery isolator
