Tags: [V] verified from a fetched or search-result source, [E] estimate or general knowledge. VicRoads pages returned 403 to my fetcher, so the VicRoads facts come from search snippets. Confirm on the live page or by phone (13 11 71) before paying.

## 1. Registration

**Vessel (expired 23 Nov 2025, about 11 months)**
- [V] Rego is 12 months. A vessel must be renewed within 3 months of expiry or the rego is cancelled and you must start a new registration. Cancelled rego means "How to register a vessel", not a renewal. https://www.vicroads.vic.gov.au/registration/new-registration/register-a-vessel
- [V] Renewal after more than 90 days is not available online, so this boat is a cancelled, new registration. Treat the old rego number as gone. [E] A new number may be issued, though VicRoads sometimes lets you request the old one back.
- [V] Transfer applies to a currently registered vessel: buyer and seller both sign the VicRoads vessel transfer form, and the buyer lodges it within 14 days of possession. The fee is $21.50 for a powered recreational vessel. https://www.vicroads.vic.gov.au/registration/buy-sell-or-transfer-a-vehicle/vessel-registration-and-transfer-fees
- [E] A lapsed rego cannot be transferred. The group (or one named owner) registers the boat fresh as owner. Expect to bring ID, proof of ownership (a signed receipt or bill of sale, plus the old rego papers or HIN/plate details), and proof of address. Fresh registration probably means the new registration fee, which was $98.80 last year. [E] No survey is needed for a pre-existing recreational vessel, but confirm. [E] It is probably in-person or phone only, not online.
- [E] Registration is only needed to use the boat on Victorian waters (Marine Safety Act 2010, "uses or is capable of using an engine in Victorian waters"). A boat kept on land is unlikely to be fined, but it can't be launched, even for a test run, until registered. Check with STV. https://www.vicroads.vic.gov.au/registration/new-registration/register-a-vessel
- [E] The Marine Safety Act penalty for operating an unregistered vessel is a few hundred dollars or more.
- Safe Transport Victoria (STV) writes the rules; VicRoads/DTP is its agent for rego and fee collection. https://safetransport.vic.gov.au

**Trailer (Dunbier tandem)**
- [V] Expired less than 3 months can be renewed. Expired more than 3 months means it needs an inspection (roadworthy/VicRoads-authorised checking) before re-registering. https://www.vicroads.vic.gov.au/registration/new-registration/register-a-trailer-or-caravan
- [V] An unregistered vehicle permit (UVP) lets you drive or tow an unregistered vehicle on the road for a limited period and purpose. It does not allow parking on a roadway. Permits can only be changed or cancelled before the start date. https://www.vicroads.vic.gov.au/registrations/permits/unregistered-vehicle-permits
- [V] Driving or towing unregistered on the road can attract fines (search snippet: up to about $1,427 for vehicles).
- [E] The trailer must be registered before it goes on the road with the boat. A UVP (about one day to a few weeks) is the way to take it to a mechanic or inspection station. Check whether the trailer's own rego is still current. If it is, the transfer is simple.
- [E] If the trailer's rego is current, put it in the buyers' names via the normal vehicle transfer.

## 3. Maintenance intervals
- Yamaha outboard annual service. Engine oil, gearcase oil and impeller check every 100 hours or annually, whichever comes first. Source for the general Yamaha schedule: https://yamahaonlineparts.com/yamaha-outboard-service-schedule [V]. [E] Applies to 2-stroke: gearcase oil, plugs, fuel filter, anodes, and grease nipples annually.
- Water pump impeller. The Yamaha schedule says inspect annually or every 100 hours, replace every 300 hours or 3 years [V]. [E] Many mechanics replace every 1-2 years, and always on a boat that has sat unused. Do it on this one.
- Trailer wheel bearings. [E] Service or repack at least annually, or every 10,000 km; always after immersion, and after long storage. No source found.
- Hand flares. [V] Expire 3 years from manufacture. https://safetransport.vic.gov.au/news/drop-off-your-old-flares/ (Victoria has a flare disposal program).
- Fire extinguisher. [V] Inspect at least every 6 months (pressure or weight) and recharge if needed. https://royallifesavingwa.com.au/your-community/your-safety/out-and-about/boating-equipment-expiry [E] The extinguisher's own date label also matters.
- EPIRB. [V] Battery and registration expiry must be current. Registration is free with AMSA and must be updated every 2 years. https://beacons.amsa.gov.au or 1800 406 406. [E] Battery life is about 5 years. An EPIRB is required beyond 2 nm from the coast.
- Inflatable lifejackets. [V] Service as per manufacturer's instructions. [E] Usually annual, with the cylinder and bobbin replaced on schedule. Guidance: https://handbook.safetransport.vic.gov.au/
- Vic boat rego cycle. [V] 12 months. https://www.vicroads.vic.gov.au/registration/new-registration/register-a-vessel
- Marine (boat) licence. [E] A Victorian recreational boat licence has no renewal cycle I could verify (search found nothing). Confirm with VicRoads or the STV handbook.
- Handbook: Vic Recreational Boating Safety handbook, https://handbook.safetransport.vic.gov.au/
